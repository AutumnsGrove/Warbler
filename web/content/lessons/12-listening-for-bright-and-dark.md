## Warm-up

- (60s) Straw or lip bubbles with a soft "oo". Slide up and down 5 times.

## Learn

Two voices can sing the same note and still sound different. The difference is resonance: how the shape of your mouth and throat colours the sound.

**Pitch comes from your voice folds. Resonance comes from the space above them.** Change your mouth shape and the sound changes, even if the pitch stays exactly the same.

People often describe resonance as **bright** or **dark**. These are words for what you hear, not measurements. A forward tongue, as in "ee", tends to sound brighter. A tongue pulled back, as in "oo", tends to sound darker.

This lesson is about hearing the difference and controlling it. It does not say one is better. You decide later where you want to be.

Everything here comes from your tongue, lips and jaw. Do not squeeze or push your throat.

## Practice

The pitch line should stay flat through every step. That proves only the resonance is changing.

1. **Three vowels, one note.** Hold your base note and say "oo", then "ah", then "ee", about 3 seconds each. Keep the pitch line flat. Repeat 3 times. (90s)
2. **Replay.** Tap replay and listen to your last attempt. Which vowel sounds brightest? Which sounds darkest? (30s)
3. **Slide the vowel.** Hold the note and glide slowly from "oo" to "ee" and back. The pitch line stays flat while the sound changes. (60s)
4. **Same vowel, two shapes.** Say "ah" with a relaxed, open mouth. Then say it with the tongue slightly forward and the corners of the mouth gently spread. Same note. Replay both. (90s)
5. **Words on one note.** Say "me", "may", "more" and "moo" on your base note. Replay them. (60s)
