## Warm-up

- (60s) Relaxed breathing: in for 3, out for 4, five times, one hand on your belly.

## Learn

Both talking too loudly and too softly can stress a voice, according to the US National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders. A comfortable volume carries to the person you are talking to without pushing from your throat.

A hospital voice sheet from Cambridge describes volume practice built on relaxed breathing. You vary the loudness of simple sounds smoothly, with the breath doing the work and the throat staying loose.

Warbler draws your volume as a smooth line. Your goal in each exercise is a smooth rise and fall, with no bumps.

## Practice

1. **Waves.** On a long "sh", swell louder then softer, like a wave coming in and going out. Do it 5 times. (60s)
2. **Passing vehicles.** On "v", then "m", then "n", start quiet, grow loud, and fade out again, like a vehicle driving past. Two passes each. (90s)
3. **Mosquito.** On "z", swell and fade the same way, as if an insect flew near and away. Twice. (30s)
4. **Radio dial.** On "wow", turn the volume up slowly as if turning a dial, then back down. Do it 4 times. (60s)
5. **Three levels.** Say "I'll see you on Monday" at three volumes: quiet, comfortable, and carrying (as if to someone across a room). Keep your belly breath and loose throat for all three. Warbler shows the volume of each. (90s)
6. **Pick your comfortable.** Say the sentence once more at the level you would use in everyday conversation. (30s)

- A picture of your smooth swells, and the volume of your three levels.
