## Warm-up

- (60s) Straw or lip bubbles with a soft "oo". Slide up and down 5 times.

## Learn

Every word starts somewhere. A **hard start** is a sudden push, like a tiny cough before the vowel in a word such as "apple". An **easy start** lets the sound fade in smoothly.

Easy starts are a standard part of voice training in NHS leaflets, which use them to help a voice that is forced or strained. They begin with gentle humming, then add words that start with soft sounds like "m" and "n".

Warbler shows how fast your sound rises from silence. A smooth, gradual rise means an easy start. A sharp jump means a hard one. Neither is a score. It is a way to see what your starts are doing.

This lesson is optional. Keep everything light. If you feel yourself pushing, you are going too hard.

## Practice

1. **Hum.** Hum gently on your natural note. Feel the buzz behind your lips. (30s)
2. **Chant.** Chant "ma, me, my, more" on that note, lightly. (45s)
3. **Stretch the m.** Prolong the first sound: "mmmmaaa, mmmmeee, mmmmooore". Watch the rise on the graph stay smooth. (45s)
4. **Soft phrases.** Chant these quietly, stretching every "m" and "n": "Nine new moons in a morning." "Mum and Nan name more men." (60s)
5. **Vowel starts.** Say "ah", "ee" and "oh". Begin each with a tiny breath, a very light "h", then slide into the vowel. Compare the rise with a hard start. (60s)
6. **Words.** Say "open", "easy", "every" and "answer" with an easy start. (60s)

## Done

- Rise time of each start, shown as a small picture. Smoother means a gentler start.
