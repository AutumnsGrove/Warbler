## Warm-up

- (60s) Straw or lip bubbles with a soft "oo". Slide up and down 5 times.

## Learn

Two things about your tongue and jaw shape the sound most:

- **Front or back.** A tongue that sits forward (as in "ee") raises the second resonance, called F2. A tongue pulled back (as in "oo") lowers it.
- **Open or closed.** A wider jaw opening raises the first resonance, called F1. Compare "ah" (open) with "ee" (nearly closed).

Together, these two resonances carry most of what makes a vowel sound like that vowel. Later lessons show them on a chart.

Keep your tongue relaxed, never tense. Think of moving it, not squeezing it.

## Practice

Pitch stays flat on your base note throughout.

1. **Front and back.** Say "ee", then "oo", 3 seconds each. Feel your tongue move forward and back. Repeat 4 times. (60s)
2. **Open and closed.** Say "ee", then "ah". Feel your jaw drop. Repeat 4 times. (60s)
3. **One vowel, two positions.** Say "ah" with the tongue resting where it naturally sits. Then say "ah" with the tongue tip resting just behind your lower front teeth and the body of the tongue gently forward. Replay both. (90s)
4. **Word list.** Say these on your base note, with a forward tongue: "key, lean, seen, tea, meet". Replay. (60s)
5. **A sentence.** Say "We need three green beans" with a forward tongue. Replay and compare it with a relaxed version. (60s)
