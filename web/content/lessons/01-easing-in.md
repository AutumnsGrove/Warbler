## Warm-up

About 2 minutes.

- (30s) Sit in an open, relaxed position: feet on the floor, legs and arms uncrossed, sitting upright without stiffness.
- (30s) Take a sip of water.
- (60s) Breathe in slowly through your nose and out through your mouth. Do this three times, letting your shoulders drop each time you breathe out.

## Learn

About 3 minutes.

**What Warbler is.** Think of Warbler as a mirror for your voice. It listens through your phone and shows two things: how high or low your voice is (pitch), and how loud it is. Every number is an approximate measurement. None of it is a score, and nothing here is a test.

**What your voice is.** Your voice is made by air passing two small folds of tissue in your throat, helped along by many small muscles. Like any muscles, they can get tired or irritated if they are asked to do too much.

**Signs to take seriously.** The US National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders (NIDCD) lists these as reasons to take notice and see a doctor:

- your voice has become hoarse or raspy
- your throat often feels raw, achy or strained
- it has become an effort to talk
- you find yourself clearing your throat again and again

**The one rule for everything in Warbler.** If you feel pain, strain, tightness or hoarseness, stop. Sip some water and rest your voice. If any of those signs keep coming back, NIDCD suggests seeing a doctor who specialises in ear, nose and throat problems (an otolaryngologist). They may refer you to a speech-language pathologist, which is a professional who works on voice and speech.

**Little and often.** Talking too loudly and too softly can both stress a voice, according to NIDCD. One NHS hospital leaflet on voice exercises suggests "little and often" rather than long sessions. That is why Warbler has a session limit and gentle break reminders, and why most lessons are about ten minutes.

## Practice

About 4 minutes. **Stop if you feel pain, strain, tightness or hoarseness. Rest and drink some water.**

This is a look-around, not a workout. The goal zones are hidden for this lesson. You are only watching.

- (60s) Say your name and one ordinary sentence in your usual, comfortable voice. Do it three times. Watch the line on the graph and the number beneath it.
- (30s) Notice the pitch number and its note name. Do not try to change anything. This is only where your voice happens to be right now.
- (60s) Say the same sentence a little quieter, then a little louder. Stay comfortable: do not push or strain. Watch the volume meter change.
- (60s) Body check. How does your throat feel right now? Easy, slightly tight, or sore? Take a moment to notice. You will have a chance to record this in a moment.

## Done

- Summary of the session.
- Feel tags (optional): how your voice felt.
- Reminder: rest your voice for a few minutes now. A sip of water is a good idea.
