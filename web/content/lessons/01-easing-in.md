## Warm-up

- (20s) Sit upright with your feet flat and your arms and legs uncrossed.
- (20s) Sip some water.
- (20s) Take three slow breaths. Drop your shoulders on each breath out.

## Learn

Warbler listens through your phone and draws two things: your pitch (how high or low your voice is) and your volume. The numbers are approximate. There are no scores.

Your voice comes from air passing two small folds of tissue in your throat, worked by small muscles. They can tire, like any muscles.

**Stop when you notice any of these.** The US National Institute on Deafness and Other Communication Disorders (NIDCD) lists them as warning signs:

- a hoarse or raspy voice
- a throat that feels raw, achy or strained
- talking that feels like effort
- clearing your throat over and over

If they keep coming back, NIDCD recommends seeing an ear, nose and throat doctor, who may refer you to a speech-language pathologist (a voice and speech specialist).

**Keep sessions short.** An NHS leaflet suggests "little and often". Warbler's session limit and break reminders follow that idea.

## Practice

No goals are shown in this lesson. You are learning how the graph responds to you.

1. **Say your name.** Watch the line draw your pitch. (30s)
2. **Hold a hum.** Hum "mm" on one comfortable note for 10 seconds. Try to keep the line flat. Do it three times. (60s)
3. **Slide.** Hum from your lowest comfortable note up to your highest, then back down. Watch the line follow you. (45s)
4. **Volume.** Say "ah" on one note. Get a little louder, then a little quieter, and keep the pitch line flat. Do not push. (60s)
5. **Your usual voice.** Say any sentence at your normal pace and volume. Note the pitch number and its note name. This is just where your voice sits today. (45s)
