## Warm-up

About 1 minute.

- (30s) Sit upright and relaxed. Sip some water.
- (30s) Let your jaw hang loose for a moment, then close your lips gently.

## Learn

About 2 minutes.

**What this exercise is.** Voice professionals call this family of exercises "semi-occluded vocal tract" exercises, or SOVT for short. The plain meaning: you make sound while your mouth is partly blocked, for example by breathing out through a straw. A hospital leaflet from the NHS explains that this builds a little gentle pressure below your voice folds, so your voice needs less muscle effort to get going.

**What the research says, and does not say.** The research is small. In one four-week study of 22 healthy adults who did straw exercises daily, the only change measured was that the voice needed slightly less air pressure to start. Other measures did not change, and the small benefit was gone within about a week of stopping. So this is a gentle way to ease into using your voice, and not a fix for anything.

**What you need.** A straw and a cup, half full of room-temperature water. No straw? You can blow gentle bubbles through loosely closed lips instead.

## Practice

About 7 minutes. **Stop if you feel pain, strain, tightness or hoarseness. Rest and drink some water.**

The pitch graph is on so you can see your sound, but there are no goals. Everything should feel easy.

1. **Bubbles.** Sit comfortably. Put the straw in the water, bending it toward you so it does not touch the bottom of the cup. Blow gentle bubbles. You should feel your belly and cheeks working a little. (30s)
2. **Add a sound.** Keep blowing bubbles and add a soft "oo". You may feel a light buzzing in your face. Do this ten times, holding the "oo" for as long as is comfortable. (90s)
3. **Louder and softer.** On the "oo", slowly get a little louder, like gently revving an engine, then softer again. Ten times. Keep it comfortable. (90s)
4. **Slides.** On the "oo", slide your sound gently from low to high, then from high to low. Ten times. Watch the smooth line on the graph. (90s)
5. **Take the straw out slowly.** Lift the straw out of the water a little more each time, keeping the same easy sound and airflow. Then try without the straw at all, keeping your throat open and your face buzzing lightly. (90s)

## Done

- Summary of the session.
- Feel tags (optional).
- The leaflet suggests short, frequent practice: about five minutes, a few times a day, and also as a way to reset when your voice feels tense. You do not need to do more than this lesson.
- Reminder: rest your voice and sip some water.
