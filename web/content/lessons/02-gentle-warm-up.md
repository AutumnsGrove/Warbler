## Warm-up

- (20s) Sit upright. Sip some water.
- (20s) Let your jaw hang loose, then close your lips gently.

## Learn

This is a "semi-occluded vocal tract" exercise, or SOVT for short. In plain words: you make sound while your mouth is partly blocked, here by blowing through a straw into water. An NHS hospital leaflet says this builds a little pressure below your voice folds, so your voice needs less muscle effort to start.

The research is small. In one four-week study of 22 healthy adults, the only change measured was that the voice needed slightly less air pressure to start, and that faded within about a week of stopping. Treat this as an easy way to start using your voice, not a fix.

You need a straw and a cup half full of room-temperature water. No straw? Blow bubbles through loosely closed lips.

## Practice

Your pitch graph is on, with no goals.

1. **Bubbles.** Put the straw in the water, bent toward you and not touching the bottom. Blow gentle bubbles. (30s)
2. **Add "oo".** Keep blowing bubbles and add a soft "oo". Hold it as long as is comfortable. Repeat 10 times. (90s)
3. **Louder and softer.** On the "oo", swell gradually louder, then softer, like gently revving an engine. 10 times. (90s)
4. **Slides.** On the "oo", slide from low to high, then from high to low. 10 times. Watch the smooth line on the graph. (90s)
5. **Remove the straw.** Lift the straw out a little more on each go, keeping the same easy sound. Then do it with no straw, throat open. (90s)

## Done

- Summary of the session.
- Feel tags (optional).
- The NHS leaflet suggests about five minutes, a few times a day. You can reuse steps 1 to 4 any time as a quick reset.
