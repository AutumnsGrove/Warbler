## Warm-up

- (60s) Straw or lip bubbles with a soft "oo", 5 times, as in the warm-up lesson.

## Learn

Your range is the span from the lowest to the highest sound you can make comfortably. Everyone's is different, and there is no right size. Warbler needs to know yours so later lessons can set exercises around it.

**Hz** is how many times your voice folds vibrate each second. A higher number is a higher sound.

**Note names** are a letter plus a number, like G3 or A4. Double the Hz and you go up one octave: A3 is 220 Hz and A4 is 440 Hz. Warbler shows both, so you can pick up the note names as you go.

**A semitone** is the smallest step on a piano. There are 12 in an octave. Warbler will use semitones to measure distances, because they mean the same thing for any voice.

Stay in the comfortable part of your range. If your voice cracks or flips, carry on gently.

## Practice

1. **Find your low end.** Hum "mm" and slide down until you reach your lowest comfortable note. Hold it for 2 seconds. Do it 3 times. (60s)
2. **Find your high end.** Hum "mm" and slide up to your highest comfortable note. Hold it for 2 seconds. Do it 3 times. (60s)
3. **Full slide.** On "oo", slide slowly from your lowest note to your highest and back down. Do it twice. (60s)
4. **Read your range.** Warbler shows your lowest and highest notes, in Hz and note names, and the distance between them in semitones. (30s)
5. **Find your speaking pitch.** Say any sentence in your usual voice. Warbler marks where the middle of your speaking pitch falls inside your range. (45s)

## Done

- Your range, in Hz, note names and semitones.
- A button: "Use as my range". It saves your lowest and highest notes, and later lessons use them to place exercises. You can redo this lesson any time.
