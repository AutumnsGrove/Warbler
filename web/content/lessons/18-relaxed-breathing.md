## Warm-up

- (60s) Straw or lip bubbles with a soft "oo". Slide up and down 5 times.

## Learn

Speech runs on breath. If you run low, the throat tends to compensate by squeezing, and that tires the voice. NHS leaflets describe this as one source of strained or weak volume.

The pattern to aim for is calm and low. As you breathe in, your belly moves out a little and your ribs widen. As you breathe out, your belly returns. Your shoulders stay still.

Warbler's volume meter and a timer show how long and how steadily you can make a sound on one breath. The times vary from day to day, and there is no target.

If you feel dizzy at any point, sit and breathe normally until it passes.

## Practice

1. **Set up.** Sit with your feet flat and slightly back under the chair, back straight but not stiff. Put one hand on your belly and one on your ribs. Breathe out fully. (30s)
2. **Count the breath.** Breathe in through your nose for a count of 3, and out through your mouth for a count of 3. Do it 5 times, smooth and relaxed. (60s)
3. **Longer out.** Breathe in for 3, out for 4. Over 5 breaths, stretch the out-breath: 4, 5, 6. (60s)
4. **Long "s".** Breathe in for 3, then make a quiet "sssss" and keep it steady for as long as is comfortable. Warbler times it. Do it 3 times. (90s)
5. **Soft and loud.** Breathe in, then make a steady "sss" that gets louder slowly, then softer. Do it twice. (60s)
6. **Count aloud.** Breathe in and count aloud from 1 to 21 in groups of seven. Take a small top-up breath after each group. (60s)
7. **Lists.** Breathe in and say the days of the week, then the months, in one breath each if comfortable. Take a top-up when you need one. (60s)

## Done

- Your longest steady "s" for the session.
- Feel tags (optional).
